# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A mix of two audiences: peers and friends who follow Alix's writing regularly, and a general web audience discovering her work through search or social. Regular readers want quick access to new posts and familiar navigation. First-time visitors need a clear sense of who writes here and what to expect.

Reading contexts vary: quick scanning between tasks, focused long-form reading, and occasional reference/revisiting older posts.

## Product Purpose

Allizon.io is a personal writing site — a home for essays, reviews, and short posts that lives outside any platform. It exists to publish writing on its own terms: no algorithm, no paywall, no noise between the reader and the words. Success looks like someone arriving through search or a link, reading for a few minutes, and leaving with a sense that they've encountered a real person's voice.

## Brand Personality

Warm, personal, conversational. The site should feel like sitting down with a friend who has something interesting to say — not like reading a publication or a corporate blog. The personality is self-aware but not self-serious, thoughtful but not academic.

Three words: **warm, direct, curious**.

## Anti-references

No generic blog templates. Avoid the Medium clone look, default theme aesthetics, and any pattern that says "this is a stock blog" rather than "this is Alix's site." No SaaS tropes (hero metrics, gradient text, numbered section markers). No decoration for its own sake.

## Design Principles

1. **Write first, design second** — The design exists to serve the writing. Layout, typography, and whitespace create a frictionless reading experience. Every visual decision starts with the question: does this help someone read?

2. **Warmth through substance, not decoration** — Warmth comes from voice, pacing, and material choices (paper-like surfaces, thoughtful spacing, intentional typography), not from decorative flourishes, beige backgrounds, or faux-personality gimmicks.

3. **Own your voice** — No templates, no stock patterns. Every design choice should feel intentional and personal, like the writing itself. If it could belong to any blog, it doesn't belong here.

4. **Respect the reader's attention** — Minimal noise, clear hierarchy, predictable navigation. The reader came for the words; get out of their way. Every element earns its place.

5. **Evolve deliberately** — The design grows with the writing. Changes serve the content, not trends. A personal site isn't a finished product; it's a living space that shifts as the author does.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance as a baseline. Keyboard-navigable, readable contrast ratios, reduced-motion support for animations. No concessions on readability for the sake of aesthetic — clean hierarchy benefits everyone.
